# ADR-025 — `mav-frame` and `mav-codec` are parked, not deleted

Status: accepted.

## Context

Both crates were completed in M0 and are correct. Neither has a caller.

`mav-frame` holds CRC-8/16/32, a reassembler, and a typed reader. `mav-codec` holds the explicitly
admitted open Bluetooth SIG profile decoders. They were built for a host-side decode path that
ADR-016 and ADR-017 then moved: device knowledge now lives inside signed WebAssembly artifacts, and a
Wasm guest cannot reach a host crate. ADR-020 confirmed the same thing for reassembly — a connector
reassembles its own frames, because the frame layout is device knowledge.

The connectors do not go without: `whoop-protocol` in the connectors repository carries its own CRCs
and its own `Deframer`, compiled into the artifact.

So this is not duplication to be merged. It is two complete crates on the wrong side of a boundary
that moved after they were written.

The SIG-profile path is the one that might genuinely return. `docs/platform.md` still describes a
standard Heart Rate Measurement source admitted without a connector, and `mav-codec::standard` is
exactly that decoder, pinned by `fixtures/standard/hr_measurement_v1.json`. It is unbuilt, not
unwanted.

## Decision

Both crates leave the workspace and move to `attic/`, with their fixtures. They keep compiling
nothing and are excluded from every gate. `tools/check_deps.py` and the crate tables drop them.

They are **parked, not deleted**, and the distinction is deliberate: deleting them would make the
SIG-profile path a rewrite, and git history is a worse place to find working code than a directory
that says what it is. `attic/README.md` states the condition for re-admission — a bundled SIG-profile
source, or a host-side transport that is not connector-mediated — and that re-admission means moving
the crate back into `core/crates/` and restoring its workspace membership and dependency edge, not
copying fragments out.

## Consequences

The workspace drops from seventeen crates to fifteen, and two crates stop appearing in every
`cargo test --workspace` a reader might mistake for evidence that they are live. The documents stop
describing a host decode path that no longer exists.

Nothing is orphaned: the crates, their tests, and their fixtures move together, and no reference to
them survives outside `attic/` and the ADRs that record their history.
