# ADR-004: A canonical timeline that never interpolates

## Context

Realtime and historical data from a strap arrive out of order, duplicated, and stamped by a device
clock that is sometimes wrong. Something has to merge them into one series, and the tempting
shortcuts are to fill gaps by interpolation and to fix bad clocks by rewriting timestamps. Both
shortcuts destroy information: an interpolated sample is indistinguishable downstream from a
measured one, and a rewritten timestamp cannot be re-derived when the correction logic improves.
The prior codebases also supplied a concrete dedup lesson: keying RR intervals on
(device, ts, rr_ms) collapses two equal intervals in the same second into one, silently deleting a
zero-difference heartbeat and biasing RMSSD high.

## Decision

mav-timeline orders, deduplicates, clock-corrects, and merges into a canonical series under two
absolute rules: it never interpolates, and it never mutates a raw timestamp. Clock corrections are
stored as ClockMap segments mapping device time to wall time, with affected samples flagged and
their raw device_time preserved. Implausible timestamps fall back to BLE capture time, flagged.
The RR dedup key includes an in-second sequence tiebreaker, (device, ts, rr_ms, seq), held by an
invariant test.

## Consequences

Every value downstream of the timeline is a measurement, so a gap in a chart is a true statement
that no data exists there, and analytics never compute over invented inputs. Corrections are
reproducible: the same raw input re-synced yields the same ClockMap, and a better correction
algorithm can be applied retroactively because the raw timestamps survive. The cost is that gaps
are visible and consumers must tolerate them, which we regard as the honest behaviour rather than
a limitation.
