# ADR-034: A calibrated ECG, shown as a waveform

## Status

Accepted. Revises parts of [ADR-033](ADR-033.md).

## Context

ADR-033 froze the ECG contract before any of it had met a strap. Four of its assumptions turned
out to be wrong once a WHOOP MG was actually driven end to end, and the errors compound: each one
individually looks like a detail, and together they produce a result screen that shows a rhythm
label, three percentages and nothing else, backed by an uncalibrated trace and a confidence figure
that reads 100% whatever the model says.

**The stream it was built on is the wrong one.** ADR-033 names "the WHOOP MG raw analog-front-end
stream, including its 100 Hz single-lead ECG channel". That stream is a diagnostic feed. Its
electrode channel does not produce a usable lead: a real capture of it, finger firmly on the
electrode, has a standard deviation of 21.6 counts where a genuine reading has 99, and the
detector finds one R peak in 45 seconds. The ECG proper is recorded to strap flash at **500 Hz**
and retrieved afterwards, and it is a different packet type with a different sample encoding.

**The samples had no physical scale.** The stream contract declared `ecg` in `counts`. A recording
in converter counts cannot carry a calibration pulse or an honest `10 mm/mV` label, because
neither means anything without a volts-per-count factor. The report therefore printed real ECG
graph paper with no calibration mark — correct, and useless.

**Confidence was not a measurement.** `(top - second) / 0.2`, clamped, saturates at 100% for any
margin above 0.2. Three real recordings, including WHOOP's own Normal Sinus reference, all printed
`CONFIDENCE 100%` under a model whose actual posteriors were 0.66, 0.79 and 0.67.

**"No waveform" made the result unreadable.** ADR-033's packet ECG-P6 specifies "result is
text-first with no waveform graph", and its decision log records keeping graphs out of the in-app
result. The consequence is a screen that asks the wearer to trust a one-word label about their
heart while withholding the only evidence a person can actually judge. Every comparable product —
including the one whose hardware this is — leads with the trace.

## Decision

### 1. The capture is the stored 500 Hz reading

The ECG capture is packet type 47 subtype `0x10`, one frame per second, 500 samples of 18-bit
signed converter counts. The raw AFE stream remains available to explicitly built probes and is
never the ECG. Capture declarations move to 500 Hz.

### 2. Samples cross the ABI in millivolts

The `ecg` stream contract is `millivolts`, not `counts`. A connector that knows its device is the
only component that can convert, so it must: it owns the hardware fact, and the core owns the
interpretation.

For the WHOOP AFE the factor is exact. The converter is 18-bit over a ±10 mV range, so

```
2^18 codes / 20 mV = 13107.2 counts per millivolt      1 LSB = 76.2939 nV
```

This was established by measuring one recording in both representations at once: our decoded
counts, and WHOOP's own PDF of the same 29 seconds, which prints at a stated 10 mm/mV on a 1 mm
grid. Median R-wave amplitude over 35 beats came to 0.842 mV printed against 11,089 counts
decoded — 13,169 counts/mV, within 0.5% of the exact ratio. The measurement identifies the
converter; the converter defines the scale, and the exact ratio is what ships.

A source whose scale is not established stays uncalibrated and is labelled as such. The report
draws a 1 mV calibration pulse only where the gain genuinely means millivolts, because a pulse
beside a relative gain is a false claim about amplitude.

### 3. Confidence is the model's own posterior

`confidence_milli` is the probability the model assigned to the class it chose. It cannot
saturate, it matches the figures printed beside it, and it asserts nothing the model did not
output.

### 4. The waveform is shown

ADR-033's "no waveform graph" is withdrawn. Both apps show the recorded trace: a preview on the
result card and a readable, scrollable trace on the detail screen, drawn on ECG graph paper at a
true time base.

Model probabilities move **out** of the in-app result and remain in the downloadable PDF. The
screen answers at a glance — rhythm, rate, and whether the checks passed. The report carries the
numbers a clinician or a curious wearer can dig into. This is a division of audience, not a
withholding: the provisional nature of the model is stated on both.

### 5. The result carries a mean heart rate

`EcgResult` gains `mean_heart_rate_bpm`, computed in the core from the stored waveform by the
admitted R-peak detector. Rate is the single most legible fact about a reading, it is what the
high- and low-rate checks are made of, and computing it in either app would put an analytic
outside the core.

## Consequences

`EcgResult` changes shape, which is a frozen-interface change: it needs a storage migration and
both native decoders updated together. The 18 pinned report artifacts are regenerated. The ECG
lane's completed plan is now partly wrong about the protocol it describes and is corrected rather
than rewritten, because the record of what was believed at the time is worth keeping.

Rate-gating a classification is **not** adopted here. WHOOP returns *Inconclusive* outside
50–120 BPM, judged on instantaneous rate rather than the mean — a 57 BPM mean reading of this
wearer was refused for dipping to 45.7 BPM on three beats. Whether Maverick should refuse the same
way is a real question about a provisional model's validated range, and it is left open rather
than settled in passing.
