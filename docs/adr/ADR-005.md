# ADR-005: Capability negotiation

## Context

Devices differ in what they produce: one strap streams RR intervals, another does not. An analytic
that needs a missing stream has to do something, and the two bad options are to compute anyway from
nothing and to vanish without
explanation. Sprinkling device checks through the analytics ("if WHOOP 4.0 then…") is how the
prior codebases handled it, and those checks rotted the moment a new device arrived.

## Decision

Every analytic declares the stream kinds it requires as data, e.g. `requires: [RrInterval]`. At
connect, the engine intersects the device manifest's declared capabilities with those requirements
and produces an availability set. The UI reads the set. Nothing downstream hardcodes a device
check. An analytic whose requirements are unmet is visibly unavailable, in the inspector as
"unavailable: missing RR", never silently absent and never computed regardless.

## Consequences

Adding a device requires no edits to any analytic, and adding an analytic requires no knowledge of
any device; the intersection is computed, not maintained. Unavailability becomes a legible,
truthful state the user can see, and the admission rule's explicit stubs (algorithms waiting on a
fixture) surface through the same mechanism. The cost is that requirements must actually be
declared and kept honest: Recovery must disappear cleanly when its RR requirement is unmet, and that
behaviour is tested directly with capability sets rather than a pretend device.
